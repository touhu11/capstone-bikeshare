Case Study: Employee Attrition Analysis

1. Introduction

Employee attrition can create significant challenges for organizations, including reduced productivity, increased recruitment and training costs, and the loss of experienced employees. The dataset examined in this project shows an employee attrition rate of approximately 27.4% (Figure 1), compared with the 17.4% benchmark cited in the original analysis.

Understanding the factors associated with employee attrition can help organizations identify potential retention challenges and make more informed workforce decisions. This project explores employee characteristics and compares two classification models, Random Forest and AdaBoost, to predict employee attrition.

2. Data Exploration

The analysis uses the IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition and Performance dataset published by Pavan Subhash on Kaggle. It contains 1,470 employee records and 35 attributes, including attrition, department, education, gender, job level, job role, marital status, job satisfaction, overtime, and stock option level.

2.1 Employee Attrition Rate

As shown in Figure 1, approximately 27.4% of employees in the dataset left the organization. This rate provides an overview of the attrition problem and motivates further investigation into employee characteristics associated with turnover.

2.2 Relationships Between Attrition and Employee Attributes

Figure 2 compares attrition rates across selected employee attributes. The differences in attrition rates across categories provide an initial view of how employee characteristics may be associated with the likelihood of leaving the organization.

The exploratory findings from the original analysis include:

  • Department: Attrition rates vary across departments.
  • Education: Attrition rates differ across education levels.
  • Gender: The observed attrition rates appear relatively similar between genders.
  • Job level: Attrition rates vary across job levels.
  • Job role: Managers and research directors are among the roles identified in the original analysis as having higher attrition rates.
  • Job satisfaction: Higher job satisfaction is associated with lower attrition rates.
  • Overtime: Employees who work overtime appear more likely to leave.
  • Stock option level: Attrition rates differ across stock option levels, with levels 0 and 3 identified in the original analysis.

These findings describe observed relationships in the dataset. They do not, by themselves, establish that any particular employee attribute causes attrition.

3. Predictive Models for Employee Attrition

This project compares two classification algorithms: Random Forest and AdaBoost. Both models are used to predict whether an employee will leave the organization.

Random Forest is an ensemble learning method that combines predictions from multiple decision trees. The final classification is determined through an aggregation of the individual tree predictions.

AdaBoost is an ensemble learning method that builds a sequence of weak classifiers. It adjusts the emphasis placed on previously misclassified observations so that subsequent classifiers can focus on those observations.

The two models are evaluated using accuracy, precision, and recall. These metrics provide different perspectives on classification performance.

4. Model Evaluation and Comparison

4.1 Classification Performance by Job Satisfaction and Tenure

Figures 3 and 4 compare the classification performance of Random Forest and AdaBoost for selected combinations of job satisfaction and tenure. Table 1 summarizes the precision and recall results across the four job satisfaction levels and two tenure categories.

Table 1 shows that Random Forest generally achieves higher recall across the job satisfaction and tenure combinations. For example, when job satisfaction is 1 and tenure is 0, Random Forest has precision and recall of 0.82, while AdaBoost has precision of 1.00 and recall of 0.12. This illustrates the difference between identifying predicted attrition cases accurately and identifying a larger proportion of actual attrition cases.

4.2 Overall Model Performance

Tables 2 and 3 compare the training and testing performance of the two classification models. On the test data, both models achieve an accuracy of 0.83. However, their precision and recall results differ.

Random Forest achieves test precision of 0.87 and recall of 0.52. AdaBoost achieves test precision of 1.00 and recall of 0.43. Thus, Random Forest identifies a greater proportion of actual attrition cases, while AdaBoost produces a higher proportion of correct predictions among the cases it classifies as attrition.

5. Model Recommendation

Based on the test results, Random Forest is the preferred model when the primary objective is to identify employees who may leave the organization. Its test recall of 0.52 is higher than AdaBoost's 0.43, meaning that it identifies a larger proportion of actual attrition cases.

However, AdaBoost achieves higher test precision (1.00 compared with 0.87). Therefore, model selection should depend on the organization's priorities. If missing employees at risk of leaving is a major concern, Random Forest offers an advantage in this comparison.

Overall, the analysis demonstrates how exploratory data analysis and classification models can support data-driven employee retention decisions.
